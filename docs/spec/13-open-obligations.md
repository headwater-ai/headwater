# 13 — Open obligations

Every decision in [9 — The decision register](09-decisions.md) is closed, and each one left work behind. This file gathers that work in one place. Nothing here blocks a decision. Most items wait on an engine that nobody has built, on a corpus that nobody has adopted, or on a measurement that nobody has run. An item whose measurement has since run stays here, with the number, until the claim it was filed against is settled.

**This file is an index, and it stopped being the place an obligation lives.** Each of the 161 items below is a document on [the obligations shelf](../obligations/), of the `obligation_record` kind. It carries an identifier, a state, two dates, and an edge to the decision or the specification that produced it. The fifth class below has no such producer, and states why. It states its context, what the corpus owes, and what would discharge it, under the three headings that the kind requires. The shelf holds 169 records. Of the 161 below, 159 stand at `current` and two stand at `discharged`. The eight that are not below stand at `discharged` too, which is the terminal state a record takes when the corpus paid what it records. A record that a ruling discharges stays on the shelf and leaves the list, and [the last section](#a-human-maintains-this-list-by-hand) states what that costs. HW-OBL-0135 and HW-OBL-0146 are discharged and still listed, against that rule, which is a cost of the same kind.

An obligation was a paragraph in a list. Nothing could carry its state, its dates or its edges, and a reader who cited one cited a position in a file. Now a citation names a document with an identifier, and a check reads the document that a citation reaches.

**The six headings of this file stay because the corpus cites them.** Twenty-one citations name one of them by its anchor, and eighteen of the twenty-one stood before this conversion. So each heading keeps the text it carried, and each section lists the records that now hold its arguments.

This is where two principles are discharged for the project as a whole. [Principle 5](00-vision-and-scope.md#design-principles) requires explicit incompleteness, so a gap is named here rather than left for a reader to find. [Principle 11](00-vision-and-scope.md#design-principles) requires that efficacy is measured and never inherited. So a claim about what Headwater achieves is published as unmeasured until an instrument reports on it.

Five classes cover all of it. The first three are the classes that the register's own index named. The fourth holds the rest, so that no item falls between the classes. The fifth is new: what the engine found about itself, filed here directly rather than on the issue tracker. [The value rule](../../.claude/commands/next-run.md#the-value-rule) states why the fifth class exists at all. The classes are a partition of the shelf, and no declaration carries them. A human keeps each item under the right heading, and [the last section](#a-human-maintains-this-list-by-hand) states what that costs.

**A recording that pins an identity, landing in the same commit that moves that identity, invalidates itself atomically and silently.** A transcript records the `lock` digest of the tree it was planned against, and `headwater probe record` refuses it whole where that digest is not the digest of the tree reading it. `f615fb86` committed `docs/probe-runs/regression-probe-transcript-for-2026-09-09.md` and edited `.headwater/overlay.yml` and `.headwater/taxonomy.lock` in one commit, so the recording was dead on arrival rather than drifting afterwards. Nothing reported it for twenty-eight days, and two mechanisms are why. `headwater generate --check` stayed green, because the refusal text **is** the derived output and it regenerates faithfully. And no check rule reads a probe result at all, so the shelf that holds this corpus's only measurement is the one shelf the check layer does not look at. Three governed documents then published measurements taken off a result that holds none, one of them at an `accepted` warrant.

The general shape is worth more than the instance: **a derived artifact that records the identity of its own inputs is only as good as the ordering of the commit that lands it**, and a gate that compares an artifact against its own regeneration cannot see the difference, because the refusal and the measurement regenerate equally well. `headwater generate` now reports a refused transcript as a refusal of the run. Two gaps stand. Nothing in the check layer reads `docs/probe-results/**`, and no mechanism here orders a recording against the taxonomy change that voids it. And **`discharged` is terminal in the `obligation` lifecycle regime**, so a record discharged on a measurement that turns out not to exist cannot be retracted by an edit that the check layer admits. HW-OBL-0010 and HW-OBL-0124 each carry a file-scoped exception for that reason, with an expiry. A regime that admits no retraction makes a wrong discharge permanent, which is the opposite of what a terminal state is for.
